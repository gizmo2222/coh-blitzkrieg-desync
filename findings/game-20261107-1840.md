## Trigger: earliest difference between the Intel and AMD simulations

Method: the timestamped `warnings` lines from 18:50 to the first mismatch, compared by content (numbers masked, network
and mismatch lines dropped), looking for lines both PCs of one vendor have and neither of the other.
Scripts: `cmp_warn.py`, `cmp_two.py` (kept in the session scratchpad; easy to redo).

- 19:07:44 CST (about minute 27 of the game, sim start 18:40:47): both AMD PCs log
  `An ability failed to cast because target validation failed: abilities\ally_air_strike_ability_strafe`
  (Player D 17:07:43.6 PT, Player C 19:07:44.3). Neither Intel PC logs it: on the Intel side the strafing run was accepted.
- 95 seconds later (19:09:19) the first squad mismatches appear. A strafe that hits on one side and never happens on the
  other changes which squads die, which shifts the squad ids the later commands refer to.
- Later strafe failures (19:32, 19:35) appear on both sides, so the ability itself is not broken; only that one cast was
  decided differently. Target validation is a position check, where a rounding difference flips a borderline answer.
- Caveat: this is the earliest difference the logs show, not proven to be the first. The 05-08 desync (Player B Intel,
  Player C AMD) shows no such line before its first mismatch (20:47:20 CST), so there the trigger left no log trace.
- The design-message logs cannot help: no timestamps, and even the two AMD PCs differ there (display-side messages).
  They do show a mod data bug: `Cannot find squad for entity ebps\races\axis_panzer_elite\vehicles\88mm_flak_36_at
  when accessing slot container` about 100,000 times per game on every PC. Worth reporting; not shown to be related.

Replay check, Player A's PC (Intel), playing Player D's recording (`temp.rec`), log `warnings.2026-11-07.20-20-24.txt`:

## Why the live Intel games left the recording: candidates

Live logs of all four PCs, aligned to game time (mm:ss from mission start 18:41:43 CST; Player C's clock -1 s). The
facts to explain: both Intel live sims left the recording at different times (Player B by 23:20, Player A between 23:20 and
26:00). Both AMD live sims match it. A playback on the Intel PC matches it too, so the sim code itself, fed the recorded
commands, gives the same result on Intel. The cause must come from something that runs only in the live game.

Ranked:

1. **Frame-rate or sim-step timing on the slow PCs.** Circumstantial only:
   - The Intel PCs are the slow ones in this game. They finished loading about 68 s after the AMD slots, and Player A logged
     a 21.3 s main-loop stall (`DispatchEvents ... 21268 ms`) at mission start. At 14:14 Player A logs the shared
     CANPRODUCE line 1.3 s after the others; at the other shared lines all four fall within about 0.4 s.
   - A playback runs no AI and no network, may be fast-forwarded, and shows a different camera, so its frame timing differs.
   - Withdrawn lead: the "Was already stealing a skeleton" lines (sync animations) looked Intel-only, right before each
     Intel divergence point. Player B logged `[mortar_target]` at 23:11.5, Player A at 23:30.6, and both logged
     `[flakvierling_sync]` at 26:52-26:55; the AMD PCs logged neither. The playbacks log them too, though, in
     `D:\CoHUser\LogFiles\warnings.2026-11-07.20-20-24.txt`, aligned by the CANPRODUCE and strafe lines (the playback
     speed varied from 0.5x to 1.9x):
     - Playback 1 (Player D's rec): `[mortar_target]` at about 23:22.
     - Playback 2 (Player A's rec): `[flakvierling_sync]` at about 26:54, and `[heavymachinegun_target]` at about 24:55
       instead of the mortar line.

     Two playbacks of the same game log different lines, and the AMD live PCs, which ran that same game, logged neither
     line. So these lines depend on the camera or rendering and say nothing about the sim.
     The `[bofor_sync]` timing (17:55.5 on Player C, 17:58.8 elsewhere, with no split) shows the same thing.
2. **The local CPU AI (runs live only; playback replays its commands instead).** Each PC runs one CPU player: Player B
   1004, Player A 1005. The AI's commands go out to everyone and are recorded, so the AI can only cause this if it changes
   sim state directly instead of through a command. Against: the two AMD PCs each ran their own AI and stayed identical
   for the whole game. For: both desyncs so far had CPU players, and the AI is the largest block of live-only code. It
   also adds CPU load on the slow PCs, which feeds candidate 1.
3. **Host role / late command handling (Player B host).** Player B left first. But Player A, not the host, left too, and their
   own recording (the stream they received) plays back as the AMD game. So delivery to them matched, unless the live sim
   ran a command at another turn than the one it recorded. The transport looks clean. Peer RELY retries are frozen
   since the lobby (no in-game retries), mdat is steady at 487-488 per minute on all four, there are no main-loop stalls
   after mission start on any PC, and the FORCED ACK lines to the host come in the same bursts on all three clients
   (9:10, 9:54, 17:20, 18:40, 21:24, 23:00, ...). One change at 18:40: after a host reliable message (forced acks on
   all clients, 80.5 s since the last one), a second message stream (`data` recv, about 488 per minute) starts on all
   four PCs and runs for the rest of the game. Player B's first odd line (19:40 CANPRODUCE, Player B only) comes one minute
   later. It starts on all four at once, so it cannot split Intel from AMD by itself.
4. **Pauses or the in-game menu.** Menu screens: Player D 00:49 and 25:28, Player A 08:52, Player C 25:35, Player B never.
   Player B left first without ever opening it, and the AMD players opened it right before the 26:00 strafe. Unlikely.
5. **Alt-tab or minimize.** No focus, minimize, device-lost or window lines in any of the four logs. No evidence either
   way; the game may simply not log it.

### Test that separates 1 from 2

One playback on Player A's PC, no other players needed. A playback has no AI and no network, so if it leaves the
recording, frame timing alone did it (1). If it does not, the cause is in live-only code (2, or 3).

1. On Player A's PC, play `INFANTRYOP.2026-11-07.19-48-37.rec` at normal speed, never fast-forward. Before starting, cap
   the frame rate low with the GPU driver's frame limiter or RTSS (about 20 fps). Keep the camera on the fighting from
   minute 22 to 27.
2. Quit after minute 27 and search that log (the skeleton lines are presentation-only, so ignore them):
   `$f = Get-ChildItem "$([Environment]::GetFolderPath('MyDocuments'))\My Games\Company of Heroes Relaunch\LogFiles\warnings.*.txt" | Sort-Object LastWriteTime | Select-Object -Last 1; Select-String -Path $f.FullName -Pattern 'stealing a skeleton|CANPRODUCE|target validation' | ForEach-Object { $_.Line.Trim() }`
3. Reading: a missing 23:20 CANPRODUCE line, or no strafe failure at about 26:00, means candidate 1. The same three CANPRODUCE lines plus the strafe failure means the playback held, so the next run is a
   4-human game on the same PCs without CPU players, past 30 minutes (clean there points to 2).

Both earlier playbacks were fast-forwarded (0.5x to 1.9x), so neither one tests 1x frame timing yet. Player B's own `temp.rec` (10.2 MB) has never been played back; one playback of
it shows whether their live sim left their own recording as well.
