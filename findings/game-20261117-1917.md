## fpu-probe 2.2
- All three: 2.2 loaded, `PIN on`, World::Simulate hooked at SimEngine.dll+6C480.
- First tick: `cw=007F pc24,near` on all three. `mx=1F80` on Players A and C and `mx=1FA0` on Player B. The extra
  bit (0x20) is the sticky "precision" status flag, not a control setting, so the rounding and denormal behaviour was
  the same on all three.
- **`fixed before=0 after=0` on all three** for the whole game: Players A and C ticks=30144, Player B ticks=30920. Every
  sim tick on every PC started and ended with `cw=007F mx=1F80`, **and the game still desynced.** So the x87/SSE
  settings of the simulation thread at tick boundaries are not the cause of this split.
- The sim thread was caught off 007F mid-tick in the usual CRT-helper pattern on all three: Player A 5 times,
  Player B 8 times, Player C 18 times (027F at MSVCR100.dll+3A4F/+367F, 177F at MSVCR100.dll+ABA3A/+ABA40, 0C7F once on
  Player A). Each went back to 007F by the next scan. Player B's nearest ones were at 20:11:02 and 20:27:50, neither
  next to the split.
- Worker threads started at RelicCOH.exe+3D30A0 switch FTZ on and off (mx 1FBA <-> 9FBA) on all three, more on
  Player B: 40 CHANGED lines, against 18 on Player A and 12 on Player C. One of Player B's was at 20:25:29-34, 10-15 s
  before the earliest difference. That is a weak lead: the same switching happened all game on all PCs without a split,
  and the probe does not know what these threads compute.
- Tick timing: a steady 8 ticks/s (40-42 per scan) on all three for the whole game. 0 slow ticks. Longest tick
  151 ms (Player A), 122 ms (Player B) and 94 ms (Player C). Longest gap 542, 365 and 373 ms. **No stall around the
  split.**
- Network: no drops, retries or nudges before the end. `RLINK -- MessageCounts` stay steady through 20:25-20:27.
- The only module loaded during the match was `symsrv.dll`, on all three at 19:45-19:48. Nothing new loaded near the split.
  Sound and render DLLs loaded and unloaded only after game over.

## Differences between Player B's PC and the other two
- **Windows 10 19045 against Windows 11 26300.** It is the only difference that sets PC-B apart from both of the
  others. Almost every system DLL differs in size between PC-B and the other two, including ntdll, kernelbase,

- NVIDIA driver: each of the three PCs has a different driver package, and Players A and C still agreed, so the driver

  which both of the others did.
- CPU vendor does **not** explain it: an Intel PC split from an Intel PC plus an AMD PC. That rules out the
  "Intel vs AMD" reading of `game-20261107-1840` as a full explanation.

## What this shows
- **The pin does not prevent the desync.** With every sim tick pinned to the same x87/SSE settings on all PCs and zero
  corrections, the game split after about 57 min. The three clean pinned games on 11-13 were not proof that the pin
  works. This game takes the place of the planned unpinned control game.
- **`GameOverDialog - normal game ending (no sync errors)` does not mean the game stayed in sync.** Check for
  `belonging to` lines involving a human, and a matching `Game Over at frame` number on every PC.
- Player B's PC has now been on the losing side of several splits (05-08 against Player C, and in the 11-07 replay
  test the first live sim to leave the recording). `game-20261107-2249` was Player C alone, so it is not always
  PC-B.
- Still open: something outside the sim thread's FPU settings. Candidates are sim work on worker threads (which are
  not pinned), a difference in Windows 10 system code that the sim calls, or the CPU AI that runs locally on each PC
  (here 1002 on PC-B).
